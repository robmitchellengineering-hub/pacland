Capability brief

Quantum Verification

Exact, machine-checked quantum computation on existing hardware.

We run a quantum circuit exactly on existing hardware and return the answer together with a machine-checked proof that the answer is correct.

The problem

Quantum devices are getting bigger, but their answers are hard to trust. A naive simulator needs a memory slot for every possible outcome, so it runs out of memory around 30 qubits. The standard alternative, sampling, returns estimates with error bars and never an exact answer. Both failures are invisible until the wrong answer ships. Then it lands in a design loop, a chemistry prediction, or a hardware claim.

How we solve it

We store the quantum state as a tensor network rather than a list of outcomes. For circuits with low entanglement, the network stays small, so the computation is exact and fast. Its exactness is a theorem, checked by a proof assistant.

  1. Exact computation. Our simulator reproduces quantum circuits bit for bit, verified against the standard method on every benchmark, and passes the naive limit: a 100,000-qubit state in about six seconds.
  2. Machine-checked correctness. The method rests on a theorem checked in Lean: the entanglement across any cut is at most the size of that cut, and reaches it exactly for maximally entangled states. When a result is labeled exact, a machine has verified it.
  3. A certificate. Each computation produces a certificate with the result, a fingerprint, the evidence of exactness, and a one-line command that re-runs the check. A tampered certificate is refused, and so is a submitted result that disagrees with the certificate.

What you receive

For each computation: a certificate file, the exact result, the evidence (bond dimension and theorem reference), and a re-run command that confirms the certificate offline, without trusting whoever served it.

Scope, stated plainly

  • Exact certificates cover circuits up to 20 qubits in full. Beyond that we certify the evidence (bond dimension) rather than the full outcome list.
  • Low-entanglement circuits only. A high-entanglement circuit is flagged, not certified.
  • We sell verification and exactness. We do not claim to make quantum computing faster.
