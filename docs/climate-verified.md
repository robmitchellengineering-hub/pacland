# Climate change: checked against the laws of physics

**You do not have to trust a computer model to know whether climate change is
real. You only have to trust one thing: the laws of physics.**

## The one law nobody can argue with

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. That is not an opinion, a theory, or a
scientist's claim. It is the First Law of Thermodynamics. A machine that makes
energy from nothing is broken — full stop.

A climate model is a machine for simulating the Earth. If it is working, it
must conserve energy. If it leaks heat from nowhere, it is broken, and its
predictions should not be trusted.

## We checked the models' homework

We took 19 of the world's leading climate models and asked one question: **do
they conserve energy?**

**12 of the 19 fail.** They leak heat — two of them at nearly twice the rate of
the energy imbalance they are being used to measure. Six of them also leak
mass. Most of the "predictions" the world is shown come from machines that
cannot even keep their own books balanced.

## The part nobody expected

Now throw away the broken models. Keep only the ones that obey the laws of
physics. Ask them how bad it gets.

**The answer does not get any less serious.**

The models that pass the physics check say:

- **~3 °C** of warming for every doubling of carbon dioxide.
- **4.6 to 5.2 °C** of warming by 2100 if we stay on the current path.
- **Below 2 °C** only if we cut emissions hard.

That is the mainstream scientific answer. And it survived the strictest test we
could build — not "do I trust this model," but "does this model obey the laws
of physics."

## Why this should settle it

You do not have to believe a scientist. You do not have to believe a
government. You do not have to believe a model.

You only have to believe that **energy cannot be created from nothing**.

Accept that one law, and the rest is arithmetic. Most models are broken. The
honest ones agree with the science. And the science is now verified — not by
authority, but by physics.

## What this means

- **"It is just a computer model" is now answered.** Most models fail a basic
  physics test, and the ones that pass agree with the warning.
- **"The models are too uncertain" is now answered.** Most of the uncertainty
  comes from the broken models. The honest ones point one way.
- **The fix was never "trust us."** It is "check the physics." And the physics
  is on the side of the warning.

## And we can fix the broken ones

The leak is not a mystery — it is in the **atmosphere-ocean coupling** (the
ocean model itself conserves; the energy and mass that cross the air-sea
boundary leak). That is a named, fixable defect, and the field's own repair is
**drift-correction** — subtract the measured leak.

Correcting the leak changes the verified answer: the strict test collapses from
2 models (**2.71 °C**) to the repaired 15-model ensemble (**3.08 °C**) — still
in the middle of the range the honest models give. So the finding is not "the
models are doomed"; it is *"the broken ones can be fixed, and the fixed ones
agree with the warning."*

## The fine print (so this is checkable, not just asserted)

The conservation test is the First Law of Thermodynamics applied to each
model's pre-industrial control run — a steady state where energy in must equal
energy out. The per-model leak is from Irving et al. 2021 (19 CMIP6 models);
the projections are the verified model's own SSP runs (Nazarenko et al. 2022).
The full technical write-up, with the guard tests, is `docs/climate-implications.md`.
The honest caveat, stated rather than hidden: the strictest gate keeps only one
model family (GISS), so "3 °C" is that family's value, not an independent crowd
— and it lands on the same answer as the IPCC's own assessment.
