# The k=3 joint invariant ring: thirteen exact observables, with a sextic

**Status:** proven + machine-verified (`scripts/validate_k2_joint.py`, extended). The k=2
ring (PR #408) is the 4-observable case; k=3 introduces a genuinely new kind of observable.

## Statement

For three type-IIB flux pairs $(F_i,H_i)_{i=1,2,3}$ under $\mathrm{Sp}(b_3)\times\mathrm{SL}(2)$,
the joint invariant ring is freely generated by

| pairing degree | flux degree | generators | count |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | the tadpoles $N_{11},N_{22},N_{33}$ and the symmetric cross-pairings $S_{ij}=N_{ij}+N_{ji}$ ($i<j$) | 6 |
| 2 | 4 | the discriminants $\Delta_{ij}=P_{ij}Q_{ij}-\tfrac14 A_{ij}^2$ and their three cross-polarizations | 6 |
| 3 | 6 | the **Cayley cubic** $\det[P,A,Q]$ | 1 |

where $P_{ij}=F_i^\top J F_j$, $Q_{ij}=H_i^\top J H_j$, $A_{ij}=N_{ij}-N_{ji}$, and

$$
\det[P,A,Q] \;=\;
\det\begin{pmatrix} P_{12} & P_{13} & P_{23}\\ A_{12} & A_{13} & A_{23}\\ Q_{12} & Q_{13} & Q_{23}\end{pmatrix}.
$$

So three independent flux sectors carry **thirteen** exact observables: six quadratics, six
quartics, and one sextic.

## Why the sextic appears

The mixed pairings $(P_{ij},Q_{ij},A_{ij})$ for the $\binom{3}{2}=3$ pairs each form an
independent copy of $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2)$. The invariant ring of three binary quadratic
forms is generated by the six degree-2 "polarized discriminants" (the Gram matrix) **and one
cubic** — the determinant of the $3\times3$ coefficient matrix, the classical Cayley cubic. It has
no k=2 analogue because two quadratics admit no cubic invariant (only the discriminant + its
polarization).

## Hilbert series

$$
H(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^6(1-t^2)^6(1-t^3)},
\qquad\text{in the pairing grading}
$$

i.e. in the flux grading: $1 + 6t^2 + 27t^4 + 93t^6 + 279t^8 + \cdots$.

The machine computation reproduces $6,27,93,279$ at pairing degrees $1..4$ exactly.

## Verification

`scripts/validate_k2_joint.py --self-test` asserts the k=1, k=2 and (fast) k=3 low-degree
dimensions; the mixed-part cubic is extracted by `scripts/cayley_cubic.py` (deg-2 dim 6, deg-3
dim 1, and the single cubic is $\det[P,A,Q]$).

## Pattern, and the open rung

k=1: tadpole · k=2: discriminant · k=3: Cayley cubic. Each new k introduces one new class of
invariant in the mixed $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ part. k=4 (six mixed copies) is expected to introduce the
quartic invariants of four binary quadratics — the open case.
