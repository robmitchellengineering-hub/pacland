# The k=2 joint invariant ring: four exact observables

**Status:** proven + machine-verified (`scripts/validate_k2_joint.py --self-test`).
Extends the Constraint-First theorem (the $k=1$ case) to two flux sectors.

## Statement

Let $(F_1,H_1)$ and $(F_2,H_2)$ be two type-IIB flux pairs in $H^3(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{b_3}$,
with the full duality group $\mathrm{Sp}(b_3)\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$ acting in the standard
way. Then the ring of joint (duality-invariant) polynomial observables is

$$\mathbb{C}\big[F_1,H_1,F_2,H_2\big]^{\mathrm{Sp}\times\mathrm{SL}(2)} \;=\;
\mathbb{C}\big[\,N_{11},\ N_{22},\ S,\ \Delta\,\big],$$

freely generated by

$$
N_{11}=F_1^\top J H_1,\qquad
N_{22}=F_2^\top J H_2,\qquad
S=F_1^\top J H_2+F_2^\top J H_1,
$$

$$
\Delta \;=\; (F_1^\top J F_2)(H_1^\top J H_2)\;-\;\tfrac14\big(F_1^\top J H_2-F_2^\top J H_1\big)^2 .
$$

So a compactification with **two** independent flux sectors has **four** exact observables — three
tadpole-type quadratics and one quartic cross-invariant — not one.

## Why

By Weyl's First Fundamental Theorem the $\mathrm{Sp}(b_3)$-invariants of the four vectors are the six
pairings $P,Q,N_{11},N_{12},N_{21},N_{22}$ ($P=F_1^\top J F_2$, $Q=H_1^\top J H_2$). Under
$\mathrm{SL}(2)$ these split:

- $N_{11},N_{22}$ are fixed (each is a single tadpole).
- $S=N_{12}+N_{21}$ is fixed; the antisymmetric part $A=N_{12}-N_{21}$ is *not*.
- $(P,Q,A)$ transforms as $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2)$, whose only invariant is the discriminant
  $\Delta=PQ-A^2/4$.

Hence the joint ring is exactly $\mathbb{C}[N_{11},N_{22},S,\Delta]$, independent of $b_3$ (the
$\mathrm{SL}(2)$ action on the pairings does not depend on it). The generators are algebraically
independent for $b_3\ge 4$.

## Hilbert series

At the flux grading the four generators have degrees $(2,2,2,4)$, so

$$
H(t)\;=\;\frac{1}{(1-t^2)^3\,(1-t^4)}
\;=\;1+3t^2+7t^4+13t^6+22t^8+\cdots .
$$

The machine computation reproduces $0,3,0,7$ at flux degrees $1..4$ (the $k=1$ case reproduces
$0,1,0,1$, the ring $\mathbb{C}[N]$).

## Verification

`scripts/validate_k2_joint.py --self-test` computes the invariant dimension by torus-ansatz +
$\mathfrak{sl}(2)$-annihilation at the pairings level (exact sympy rank), and asserts

- $k=1$: dims $1,1,1,1$ (the ring $\mathbb{C}[N]$), and
- $k=2$: dims $3,7,13,22$ (the ring $\mathbb{C}[N_{11},N_{22},S,\Delta]$).

## Open next rung

$k\ge 3$: the $3$ tadpoles and $3$ symmetric cross-pairings stay fixed, and the $\binom{k}{2}$ mixed
$(P,Q,A)$ triples each contribute a discriminant $\Delta_{ij}$; whether the resulting ring is freely
generated by $\{N_{ii},S_{ij},\Delta_{ij}\}$ (and whether new relations appear) is the open case.
