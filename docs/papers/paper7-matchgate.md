# The matchgate even/odd split, kernel-checked: amplitudes are even-dimensional Pfaffians (2×2, 4×4, 6×6, and the recursive general object)

**The algebraic seed of matchgate classical simulability, machine-checked in Lean 4**

*Author: Rob Mitchell · the pacland proof arm · Date: 2026-11-09 · Repository: `pacland-proof/`*

**Abstract.** Matchgates are classically simulable because their amplitudes are **Pfaffians** — signed
sums over perfect matchings, equivalently square roots of determinants — and a determinant is a polynomial
in its entries, computable in polynomial time. We kernel-check in Lean 4 + Mathlib the complete
even/odd split of that correspondence, over the complex numbers. On the **even** side: the 2×2 Pfaffian
(the single perfect-matching weight) squares to the determinant (`pfaffian2_sq_eq_det`); the 4×4 Pfaffian
(the signed sum over the three perfect matchings — the exact four-qubit amplitude) squares to the
determinant (`pfaffian4_sq_eq_det`); the 6×6 Pfaffian (the signed sum over the fifteen perfect matchings —
the exact six-qubit amplitude) squares to the determinant (`pfaffian6_sq_eq_det`); and the recursive
`pfaffian`, the general even-`2m` object defined by Laplace expansion along the first row by structural
recursion on `m`. On the **odd** side: `det_antisymm_sign` (for any antisymmetric matrix,
`det A = (−1)ⁿ det A`) and its corollary `det_antisymm_odd_eq_zero` (ANY odd-dimensional antisymmetric
matrix has determinant 0, proved structurally, no cofactor computation). The infrastructure
`det_fin_four` / `det_fin_five` (the general 4×4 and 5×5 determinants Mathlib was missing) supplies the
cofactor ladders. Together these are the even/odd split behind matchgate simulability: the Pfaffian is an
even-dimensional object whose square is the determinant, and odd dimension kills it. All are checked by
the Lean kernel with no `sorry`, no `axiom`, no `admit`, no `native_decide`. What is **not** proved: the
general even-n identity `det A = Pf(A)²` (Cayley) and the full matchgate ↔ free-fermion correspondence
(the known Valiant / Jozsa–Miyake theorem) — the smallest three even cases, the recursive object, and the
general odd-dimensional no-go are kernel-checked, not the general induction.

## 1. What this is

A matchgate is a two-qubit gate whose action is generated by a quadratic free-fermion Hamiltonian. The
family matters for one reason: **matchgate circuits are classically simulable in polynomial time**
(Valiant 2002; Jozsa–Miyake 2008; the free-fermion correspondence of Terhal–DiVincenzo and Knill). The
classical algorithm is the one that makes non-interacting fermions easy: the amplitude of a matchgate
circuit is a **Pfaffian** of an antisymmetric matrix — the signed sum over perfect matchings — and by
Cayley's identity a Pfaffian squares to a determinant, which is a polynomial in the entries and computable
in polynomial time.

A full formalization of "matchgate circuits are simulable" needs the whole chain — the matchgate ↔
free-fermion correspondence, the Wick/Grassmann algebra, the Pfaffian as a sum over matchings, and
Cayley's `Pf(A)² = det(A)` for every even antisymmetric matrix. That is a long ladder. What we check here
is the **bottom of the ladder, at every rung the tooling now reaches**:

1. the Pfaffian *is* a polynomial (a perfect-matching sum) and its square *is* a determinant — the 2×2,
   4×4 and 6×6 cases, the exact two-, four- and six-qubit amplitudes;
2. the recursive `pfaffian` — the general even-`2m` object, Laplace expansion by structural recursion;
3. odd dimension kills the determinant, so the Pfaffian is an even-dimensional object — the general
   odd-dimensional theorem, proved structurally.

These facts, taken together, are the **even/odd split** of matchgates: the first is *why* the amplitude is
classically computable (a polynomial); the second is *where* matchgates live (an even number of qubits);
the third is the general object the ladder will eventually close over. Every theorem below is
kernel-checked in Lean 4 + Mathlib; the referee is the Lean kernel, which does not care about credentials.

## 2. The even side, kernel-checked (`QM/Matchgate.lean`)

The 2×2 Pfaffian is the single perfect-matching weight:

```
def pfaffian2 (a : ℂ) : ℂ := a
```

pinned to the matching-sum reading by a definitional theorem (`pfaffian2_is_perfect_matching`, `rfl`).

**Theorem 1 — the matchgate core (2×2)** (`pfaffian2_sq_eq_det`).

```
theorem pfaffian2_sq_eq_det (a : ℂ) :
    pfaffian2 a ^ 2 = Matrix.det !![0, a; -a, 0]
```

The square of the 2×2 Pfaffian equals the determinant of `[[0, a], [−a, 0]]` — `Pf² = det = a²`.

**Theorem 2 — the four-qubit amplitude (4×4)** (`pfaffian4_sq_eq_det`). The 4×4 Pfaffian
`pfaffian4 a b c d e f = a·f − b·e + c·d` is the signed sum over the three perfect matchings; its square
equals the determinant of the 4×4 antisymmetric matrix `[[0,a,b,c],[−a,0,d,e],[−b,−d,0,f],[−c,−e,−f,0]]`:

```
theorem pfaffian4_sq_eq_det (a b c d e f : ℂ) :
    pfaffian4 a b c d e f ^ 2 =
      Matrix.det !![0, a, b, c; -a, 0, d, e; -b, -d, 0, f; -c, -e, -f, 0]
```

**Theorem 3 — the six-qubit amplitude (6×6)** (`pfaffian6_sq_eq_det`). The 6×6 Pfaffian `pfaffian6` is the
signed sum over the **fifteen** perfect matchings (a fifteen-term polynomial in `a … o`); its square equals
the determinant of the corresponding 6×6 antisymmetric matrix — the exact six-qubit matchgate amplitude,
the next even rung after the 4×4.

**Theorem 4 — the recursive general object** (`pfaffian`). The general even-`2m` Pfaffian is defined by
Laplace expansion along the first row, by structural recursion on `m`:

```
def pfaffian : (m : ℕ) → Matrix (Fin (2 * m)) (Fin (2 * m)) ℂ → ℂ
  | 0, _ => 1
  | m + 1, A =>
      ∑ j : Fin (2 * m + 1), (-1 : ℂ) ^ (j : ℕ) * A 0 (Fin.succ j) *
        pfaffian m (A.submatrix (fun i => Fin.succAbove (Fin.succ j) (Fin.succ i))
                                (fun i => Fin.succAbove (Fin.succ j) (Fin.succ i)))
```

This is the general even-dimensional object behind `pfaffian2`/`pfaffian4`/`pfaffian6` — the definition the
general Cayley induction will close over. (The induction itself is the open rung, §5.)

**Infrastructure — the missing determinant lemmas.** The 4×4 and 6×6 proofs need the general `Matrix (Fin n)`
determinants that Mathlib ships for `Fin 3` but not for `Fin 4`/`Fin 5`. We supply them:

```
theorem det_fin_four (A : Matrix (Fin 4) (Fin 4) ℂ) : A.det = <full 24-term expansion>
theorem det_fin_five (A : Matrix (Fin 5) (Fin 5) ℂ) : A.det = <full 120-term expansion>
```

each closed by `Matrix.det_succ_row_zero` + the `Fin.succ` numeral lemmas + `Fin.succAbove` in the simp set.

## 3. The odd side, kernel-checked

**Theorem 5 — the general sign theorem** (`det_antisymm_sign`).

```
theorem det_antisymm_sign {n} (A : Matrix n n ℂ) (hA : Aᵀ = -A) :
    A.det = (-1 : ℂ) ^ Fintype.card n * A.det
```

The single structural fact that unifies the whole split: `det A = det Aᵀ = det(−A) = (−1)ⁿ det A`.

**Theorem 6 — odd dimension kills it** (`det_antisymm_odd_eq_zero`).

```
theorem det_antisymm_odd_eq_zero {n} (A : Matrix n n ℂ) (hA : Aᵀ = -A)
    (hodd : Odd (Fintype.card n)) : A.det = 0
```

For odd `n`, `(−1)ⁿ = −1`, so `det A = −det A`, hence `det A = 0` (ℂ has characteristic 0). Proved
*structurally* — no cofactor computation — so it holds for **every** odd dimension, not just 3×3. (The
explicit 3×3 instance `det_three_antisymm_eq_zero` is the seed that instantiates it.) This is *why* the
Pfaffian — and hence matchgates — is an even-dimensional object.

## 4. Why this matters — the one-line simulability argument

Matchgates are classically simulable *because* their amplitudes are even-dimensional Pfaffians: the
amplitude is a sum over perfect matchings (a polynomial), its square is a determinant (Theorems 1–3), the
whole object only exists on an even number of qubits (Theorem 6), and the general object is the recursive
Pfaffian (Theorem 4). The kernel-checked statements are the algebraic seed of that argument — the step that
turns "a determinant is easy" into "a matchgate amplitude is easy". The rest of the classical-simulation
theorem (the free-fermion correspondence, the general `Pf(A)² = det(A)`) is the known Valiant /
Jozsa–Miyake work; here its opening move — and the full even/odd split it rests on — is formalized, not its
conclusion.

## 5. What is NOT proved (honest)

- **The general even-n identity `det A = Pf(A)²` (Cayley).** The 2×2, 4×4 and 6×6 instances are
  kernel-checked, and the recursive `pfaffian` defines the general even-`2m` object; the *induction* that
  closes `det = Pf²` over all even `n` is the remaining rung, not claimed here.
- **The full correspondence.** The complete matchgate ↔ free-fermion equivalence, and the resulting
  polynomial-time classical simulation of matchgate circuits, are the **known** theorems of Valiant (2002)
  and Jozsa–Miyake (2008). They are *cited* here, not re-proved; only the seed (the even/odd split and the
  three smallest even cases) is kernel-checked.
