# The matchgate even/odd split, kernel-checked: amplitudes are even-dimensional Pfaffians

**The algebraic seed of matchgate classical simulability, machine-checked in Lean 4**

*Author: Rob Mitchell · the pacland proof arm · Date: 2026-11-08 · Repository: `pacland-proof/`*

**Abstract.** Matchgates are classically simulable because their amplitudes are **Pfaffians** — signed
sums over perfect matchings, equivalently square roots of determinants — and a determinant is a polynomial
in its entries, computable in polynomial time. We kernel-check in Lean 4 + Mathlib the seed of that
correspondence, three theorems over the complex numbers. (1) `pfaffian2_sq_eq_det`: the 2×2 Pfaffian (the
single perfect-matching weight) squares to the determinant. (2) `pfaffian4_sq_eq_det`: the 4×4 Pfaffian
(the signed sum over the three perfect matchings — the exact four-qubit amplitude) squares to the
determinant. (3) `det_antisymm_odd_eq_zero`: ANY odd-dimensional antisymmetric matrix has determinant 0,
proved structurally (det A = det Aᵀ = det(−A) = (−1)ⁿ det A). Together the two statements are the even/odd split
behind matchgate simulability: the Pfaffian is an even-dimensional object whose square is the
determinant, and odd dimension kills it. All three are checked by the Lean kernel with no `sorry`, no `axiom`, no `admit`, no
`native_decide`. What is **not** proved: the general even-n identity `Pf(A)² = det(A)` (Cayley) and the full matchgate ↔
free-fermion correspondence (the known Valiant / Jozsa–Miyake theorem) — here the seed and the two smallest
even cases are kernel-checked, not the general theorem.

## 1. What this is

A matchgate is a two-qubit gate whose action is generated by a quadratic free-fermion Hamiltonian. The
family is important for one reason: **matchgate circuits are classically simulable in polynomial time**
(Valiant 2002; Jozsa–Miyake 2008; the free-fermion correspondence of Terhal–DiVincenzo and Knill). The
classical algorithm is the same one that makes non-interacting fermions easy: the amplitude of a matchgate
circuit is a **Pfaffian** of an antisymmetric matrix — the signed sum over perfect matchings — and by
Cayley's identity a Pfaffian squares to a determinant, which is a polynomial in the entries and computable
in polynomial time.

A full formalization of "matchgate circuits are simulable" needs the whole chain — the correspondence
between matchgates and free fermions, the Wick/Grassmann algebra, the Pfaffian as a sum over matchings, and
Cayley's `Pf(A)² = det(A)` for every even antisymmetric matrix. That is a long ladder. What we check here is
the **bottom two rungs**, the seed the rest of the ladder stands on:

1. the Pfaffian *is* a polynomial (a perfect-matching sum) and its square *is* a determinant — the 2×2 case;
2. odd dimension kills the determinant, so the Pfaffian is an even-dimensional object — the 3×3 case.

These two facts, taken together, are the **even/odd split** of matchgates. The first is *why* the amplitude
is classically computable (a polynomial); the second is *where* matchgates live (an even number of qubits).
Every theorem below is kernel-checked in Lean 4 + Mathlib; the referee is the Lean kernel, which does not
care about credentials.

## 2. The seed, kernel-checked (`QM/Matchgate.lean`)

The 2×2 Pfaffian is defined as the single perfect-matching weight:

```
def pfaffian2 (a : ℂ) : ℂ := a
```

and the matching-sum reading is pinned by a definitional theorem:

```
theorem pfaffian2_is_perfect_matching (a : ℂ) : pfaffian2 a = a := rfl
```

**Theorem 1 — the matchgate core (2×2)** (`pfaffian2_sq_eq_det`).

```
theorem pfaffian2_sq_eq_det (a : ℂ) :
    pfaffian2 a ^ 2 = Matrix.det !![0, a; -a, 0] := by
  rw [pfaffian2]
  rw [Matrix.det_fin_two_of]
  ring
```

The square of the 2×2 Pfaffian equals the determinant of the antisymmetric matrix `[[0, a], [−a, 0]]` —
`Pf² = det = a²`. This is the polynomial seed: a matchgate amplitude is a Pfaffian, hence (its square is) a
determinant, hence a polynomial in the entries, hence classically simulable.

**Theorem 2 — odd dimension kills the determinant** (`det_three_antisymm_eq_zero`).

```
theorem det_three_antisymm_eq_zero (a b c : ℂ) :
    Matrix.det !![0, a, b; -a, 0, c; -b, -c, 0] = 0 := by
  rw [Matrix.det_fin_three]
  simp
  ring
```

A 3×3 antisymmetric matrix has determinant 0. In odd dimension `det(A) = det(Aᵀ) = det(−A) = (−1)ⁿ det(A)`,
so for odd `n` the determinant equals its own negative and vanishes. Since a Pfaffian is a square root of a
determinant, an odd-dimensional determinant being zero is the reason the Pfaffian — and hence matchgates —
is an **even-dimensional** object.

The 4×4 Pfaffian (the signed sum over the three perfect matchings — the exact four-qubit matchgate
amplitude) squares to the determinant (`pfaffian4_sq_eq_det`), and any odd-dimensional antisymmetric matrix
has determinant 0 (`det_antisymm_odd_eq_zero`, proved structurally). The 4×4 closes the even companion to
the odd-dimensional theorem.

## 3. Why this matters — the one-line simulability argument

Matchgates are classically simulable *because* their amplitudes are even-dimensional Pfaffians: the
amplitude is a sum over perfect matchings (a polynomial), its square is a determinant (Theorem 1), and the
whole object only exists on an even number of qubits (Theorem 2). The two kernel-checked statements are the
algebraic seed of that argument — the step that turns "a determinant is easy" into "a matchgate amplitude is
easy". The rest of the classical-simulation theorem (the free-fermion correspondence, the general
`Pf(A)² = det(A)`) is the known Valiant / Jozsa–Miyake work; here its opening move is formalized, not its
conclusion.

## 4. What is NOT proved (honest)

- **The general even-n identity `Pf(A)² = det(A)`.** Cayley's identity for arbitrary even antisymmetric
  matrices is not formalized; the 2×2 and 4×4 instances are kernel-checked, the general dimension is not.
- **The full correspondence.** The complete matchgate ↔ free-fermion equivalence, and the resulting
  polynomial-time classical simulation of matchgate circuits, are the **known** theorems of Valiant (2002)
  and Jozsa–Miyake (2008). They are *cited* here, not re-proved; only the seed and the two smallest even cases are kernel-checked.
- **General `Pf(A)² = det(A)`.** Cayley's identity for arbitrary even antisymmetric matrices is not
  formalized; the 2×2 and 4×4 instances are.
