# Model risk certificates

Three institutional models, graded on evidence generated by a script in this repository — not on
their authors' descriptions of them.

| model | grade | why |
|---|---|---|
| **Atlanta Fed GDPNow** | **VERIFIED** | beats the no-change null on **both** targets: 75% lower MAE against the advance estimate (**0.25** ratio) and 25% lower against the settled number (**0.31**; **0.75** excluding 2020) — the edge is not an artefact of targeting the first print |
| **Smets–Wouters (2007)** | **REFUSED** | solves correctly, but its state is 41% observable from its own series and its shock scales are unidentified, so it cannot be scored out of sample |
| **CMIP6 CNRM-ESM2-1 (CNRM-CERFACS)** | **REFUSED** | drifts **+1.49 W/m²** in a pre-industrial control where the climate must be stationary — **1.9×** the Earth's energy imbalance its projections exist to represent |

Issued from `python3 scripts/issue_certificate.py --all`. Every certificate is regenerated from
measurements, so a change in the evidence changes the certificate. The issued artefacts are committed
under `certificates/`.

## What a certificate says

Five findings, each mapped to the clause of model-risk supervision it answers (SR 11-7: conceptual
soundness, data quality, implementation, ongoing monitoring, outcomes analysis; PRA SS1/23 lifecycle),
and each traceable to a file and a function:

| clause | question | status |
|---|---|---|
| Conceptual soundness | does the model solve / conserve / represent the thing it claims? | satisfied or failing |
| Data quality | is the input real-time or revised history? | satisfied or failing |
| Implementation | can the check go red on a seeded fault? | satisfied |
| Ongoing monitoring | can the model be run against new data? | satisfied or failing |
| Outcomes analysis | does it beat the null, and against which target? | satisfied, partial or failing |

**The grade is the worst finding, not an average.** One FAILING makes the certificate REFUSED. An
average would let a serious failure hide behind four satisfied rows, which is exactly the behaviour
model-risk validation exists to prevent.

## Why a certificate and not a report

A report is prose that ages. A certificate is **regenerated from its own evidence**: the GDPNow row
recomputes the vintages, the SW row re-solves the model, the CMIP6 row re-reads the drift ledger. If a
number moves, the certificate moves with it, and the diff is visible in the repository.

That is also the commercial argument: model-risk validation is a regulated obligation (SR 11-7 in the
US, SS1/23 in the UK) currently discharged in Word documents by consultants. This produces the same
artefact from the evidence, in seconds, with a provenance trail a supervisor can follow.

## What these three certificates do not claim

- They do not claim the models are wrong in general — only that **specific questions** answer
  **specific ways** on **specific evidence**, and the evidence is named.
- The CMIP6 certificate grades **energy** conservation on one model with the largest top-of-atmosphere
  drift. The model that fails the most ledgers is a different one (**BCC-ESM1**, graded worst across
  energy, mass and salt); the two are reported separately rather than conflated.
- Conservation in a control run is necessary and not sufficient; a conserving model can still be
  wrong about feedbacks.
- The SW certificate grades **identifiability and observability**, not whether the model's economics
  is right.
- The GDPNow certificate grades skill against **both** targets. The settled-number edge is real but thin
  when 2020 is dropped (ratio 0.75), and a thinning edge is the thing to watch as more quarters
  accumulate.

## Reproduce

```
python3 scripts/issue_certificate.py --all
```

Tests: `tests/test_certify.py` (9 tests) — the grade follows the findings, every dossier carries its
own evidence and limitations, and the SW certificate's numbers match the roster's measurement.

## The first VERIFIED certificate (2026-10-28)

GDPNow was QUALIFIED because its skill was measured against the **advance** estimate — the number the
nowcast was built to hit. Scoring the same 37 vintages against the **settled** number closes that gap:

| target | sample | MAE ratio vs null | GDPNow MAE | null MAE | wins |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| advance | all | **0.25** | 1.262 | 5.055 | 28/37 |
| settled | all | **0.31** | 1.544 | 4.927 | 24/37 |
| advance | excl. 2020 | 0.51 | 0.833 | 1.639 | 24/33 |
| settled | excl. 2020 | **0.75** | 1.250 | 1.675 | 20/33 |

**It beats the null on both targets and both samples**, so the edge survives the harder question. The
margin narrows sharply as the target hardens — 0.25 → 0.75 — and that narrowing is the finding, not a
footnote: most of the apparent skill is nowcasting the *first print*, and only about a quarter of it
survives to the number that is revised.

All five findings are now satisfied, so the certificate is **VERIFIED** — the first on the board.
