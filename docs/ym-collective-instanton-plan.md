## The jump being made

The toy closed `m = e^{−β}` (c = 1) by tunneling through ONE compact soft mode. The full lattice has a
CONTINUUM of soft modes (the low-momentum gluons), and `b₀ = 11N/(48π²)` is the SUM over them. So the
move from c = 1 to c = 12π²/11 IS the mode sum. Everything downstream follows from nailing that sum.

## Three steps

1. **The mode sum = the beta-function.** Enumerate the soft modes (the collective `Σ_p sin θ_p = 0` and
   its momentum harmonics) and prove the per-mode instanton contributions sum to `b₀`. This is the
   "count the low-momentum gluons" step — the sum IS the beta-function coefficient.
2. **The collective instanton trial state.** Build the configuration that tunnels through ALL modes at
   once (the dilute instanton gas) and compute its total action `= Σ_modes (per-mode action) = c·β`.
   This is the object the toy lacked: one instanton that feels every soft mode simultaneously.
3. **The variational bound.** The collective instanton's Rayleigh quotient is `e^{−cβ}` with
   `c = 12π²/11`, so `m ≤ e^{−cβ}` — the tight upper bound, closing the real sandwich (not the toy c = 1).

## The honest hard part

Step 1 is the beta-function — the sum over the gluon momentum modes. The toy had one mode; the real
lattice has a continuum. Once the mode sum is formalized as `12π²/11`, steps 2–3 are constructions on
top of it.
