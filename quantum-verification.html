Quantum Verification

Exact, machine-checked quantum computation on existing hardware, delivered with a certificate that proves the answer is correct.

We run a quantum circuit exactly on ordinary computers and return the answer together with a machine-checked proof that it is correct.

The problem

Quantum computers are getting bigger, but their answers are hard to trust. A normal computer stores every possible outcome, so it runs out of memory around 30 qubits. The usual alternative, sampling, returns estimates with error bars, never an exact answer. Both failures are invisible until the wrong answer ships.

What we do

  1. Run it exactly. We store the quantum state as a tensor network. For circuits with low entanglement the answer is exact and fast: 100,000 qubits in about six seconds.
  2. Prove it correct. A proof assistant (Lean) has checked the theorem the method rests on. When we label a result exact, a machine has verified it, not us.
  3. Certify it. Every computation produces a certificate: the result, a fingerprint, the evidence, and a one-line re‑run command. A tampered certificate, or a wrong result, is refused.

Who uses this, and how

A quantum computer’s answer is hard to check. We run the circuit exactly, on the cases where that is possible, and certify whether the answer is right.

Quantum computing companies

They build the machines and need to prove the results are real.

Example: a startup claims a new chip solved a problem. We run the same circuit exactly and certify whether the claim holds, before it becomes public.

Companies that buy quantum results

Finance, logistics, and pharma teams are offered “quantum” answers they cannot check themselves.

Example: a logistics team is handed a “quantum” optimisation result. We check it, so they do not pay for a wrong answer.

Researchers and labs

They benchmark simulations and need an independent referee.

Example: a lab reproducing a published quantum result gets a pass-or-fail on it, with the proof, rather than trusting the paper.

What you receive

A certificate file, the exact result, the evidence (the size of the representation, and the theorem reference), and a re‑run command that confirms it offline, without trusting whoever served it.

Scope, stated plainly

  • The 100,000-qubit figure is for low-entanglement circuits; there we certify the evidence, not the full outcome list.
  • A full outcome list is certified up to 20 qubits.
  • Low-entanglement circuits only. A high-entanglement circuit is flagged, not certified.
  • We sell verification and exactness. We do not claim to make quantum computing faster.
