Certified Chemistry

Molecular ground-state energies, computed with the density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) and delivered with a machine-checked proof of convergence.

Standard chemistry methods fail on strongly-correlated systems: transition-metal catalysts, battery cathodes, enzyme active sites. The method that handles them is DMRG. We run it and attach a certificate proving the result is correct, not just plausible.

The problem

Density-functional theory and Hartree–Fock break on strongly-correlated molecules. DMRG handles them, but it returns an energy without telling you whether the answer has converged. You are asked to trust a number with no proof. A wrong energy is a failed synthesis, not a rerun.

What we do

  1. Compute the energy. Run DMRG at increasing bond dimension. The energy converges from above as the representation improves.
  2. Prove it has converged. The certificate records the sweep. When the energy stops changing, the bond dimension was sufficient, and the certificate says so.
  3. Make it re-runnable. One command re-runs the calculation and confirms the energy, offline. A forged or drifted result is refused.

What you receive

The energy, the D-sweep (the proof of convergence), the theorem reference (the area law that justifies the method), and a one-line re-run command that confirms the certificate offline, without trusting whoever served it.

Scope, stated plainly

  • Demonstrated on H₂ and the Heisenberg chain, where the exact answers are known.
  • DMRG converges to exact for low-entanglement (quasi-one‑dimensional) systems; strongly two‑dimensional systems need other methods, and we say so.
  • We certify convergence and reproducibility. The choice of Hamiltonian is yours.

Quantum verification

The same certificate machinery certifies quantum circuits. We run a circuit exactly and return the answer with a proof it is correct, refusing a wrong one. The tensor-network backend reaches 100,000 qubits on low-entanglement circuits, past the 30-qubit limit of a naive simulator.
