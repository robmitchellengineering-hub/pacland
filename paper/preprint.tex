\title{Probably Approximately Correct String Theory:\\ A Verification Discipline for the Landscape}

\begin{abstract}
The string landscape contains on the order of $10^{500}$ vacua, and no experiment
distinguishes most of them, so ``unified string theory'' is two conflated
problems---\emph{verification} (does some vacuum match our universe?) and
\emph{selection} (why that one?). We separate them, and build a \emph{verification
discipline} for the first: a probably-approximately-correct certificate that bounds a
candidate's error even when the verifier is itself approximate (Theorem~\ref{thm:twolevel}),
a proof that search over the flux superpotential is \emph{not evolvable} (Denef--Douglas
uniformity + Valiant's contrapositive, measured on $380$ real SUSY vacua), a
\emph{measured} log-uniform prior for $|W_0|$ (CDF linearity $R^2 = 0.92$), a certified
\emph{verification deficit} that is total today ($m=0$ validated oracle outputs), and a
\emph{boundary map}---a taxonomy of the four senses in which the problem is
``uncomputable,'' each with its certified positive remainder. We do not claim to select
the vacuum. We claim something narrower and we believe more useful: \emph{the field
currently has no verification layer for its unverifiable theory, and this is one.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

String theory is not a single theory but a family of $\sim 10^{500}$ metastable
vacua---the \emph{landscape}~\cite{bousso,douglas,susskind}. ``Unified string theory''
decomposes into two claims the literature habitually fuses: \textbf{verification}
(there exists a vacuum matching our universe) and \textbf{selection} (a measure singles
it out). The first is checkable in principle; the second is the open measure problem.
This paper concerns the first, and does so rigorously.

Two facts make verification hard. First, the search space is astronomically large.
Second, and more fundamentally, the \emph{verifier is not ground truth}: the map from a
compactification to its low-energy observables is computed by an approximate solver,
with systematic error from $\alpha'$ corrections, non-perturbative truncations, and the
Kaluza--Klein tower. Any rigorous statement about verification must contend with an
\emph{imperfect labeler}.

We import the framework built for exactly this regime---Valiant's
probably-approximately-correct (PAC) learning~\cite{valiant1984}---and apply it to
verification itself. The result is a certificate whose two error terms are separately
estimable, three consequences (a verify/search separation, a necessary-condition
conjecture, and a measurable-physics bound), and, on running the machinery against real
data, a set of empirical findings summarized in the abstract.

\textbf{Field context.} Independently of us, the field is converging on verification
from three directions: Michael Douglas is formalizing QFT in Lean~4~\cite{douglas_lean};
PhysProver is the first LLM theorem-prover for physics~\cite{physprover}; and a 2025 line
formalizes \emph{falsification} targets at a collider~\cite{falsify}. Our contribution
is the complementary angle: guards, certificates, and measured limits---verification
for the regime where ground truth is absent.

\section{The two-level certificate}
\label{sec:cert}

Let $X$ be the observable space, $D$ a measure over $X$, $\cstar : X \to Y$ the target
(our universe's true physics), and $h : X \to Y$ a candidate's prediction. We observe
$\cstar$ only through an approximate \textbf{oracle} $\oracle$. Define
\[
g(x) = |h(x) - \oracle(x)|, \qquad e(x) = |\oracle(x) - \cstar(x)|,
\]
and the true error
\[
\operatorname{err}_D(h) = \Pr_{x\sim D}\bigl[\, |h(x) - \cstar(x)| > \eps \,\bigr].
\]
The triangle inequality and a union bound give, for any $\beta \in (0, \eps)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:split}
\operatorname{err}_D(h) \;\le\; p_g(\eps - \beta) + p_e(\beta),
\end{equation}
where $p_g(\tau)=\Pr[g>\tau]$ and $p_e(\beta)=\Pr[e>\beta]$. The two terms are
\emph{separately estimable}: $p_g$ compares two things we can both compute ($h$ and
$\oracle$), while $p_e$ is the oracle's own failure rate, estimated by validating
$\oracle$ on points where $\cstar$ is known.

\begin{theorem}[Candidate bound]
\label{thm:candidate}
For fixed $h$, with $n$ samples $x_1,\dots,x_n \sim D$ and empirical disagreement rate
$\hat p_g$, with probability $\ge 1-\delta_s$,
\[
p_g(\eps - \beta) \;\le\; \hat p_g + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta_s)}{2n}} .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Oracle bound]
\label{thm:oracle}
On $m$ points where $\cstar$ is known, with empirical exceedance $\hat p_e$, with
probability $\ge 1-\delta_e$,
\[
p_e(\beta) \;\le\; \hat p_e + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta_e)}{2m}} .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Two-level Valiant certificate]
\label{thm:twolevel}
With probability $\ge 1 - \delta_s - \delta_e$,
\[
\operatorname{err}_D(h) \;\le\;
\hat p_g + \hat p_e
+ \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta_s)}{2n}}
+ \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta_e)}{2m}} .
\]
\end{theorem}

The certificate is the tuple $(\eps, \beta, n, m, \hat p_g, \hat p_e, \delta_s,
\delta_e)$. This is verification in the presence of an imperfect labeler---the
generalization of PAC's clean oracle that string theory actually requires.

\section{Verification without learnability; search with it}
\label{sec:verify}

Theorem~\ref{thm:twolevel} never invokes the complexity of the hypothesis class:
\emph{verifying what you are handed is cheap}, a Bernoulli-rate estimate. \emph{Search}
is different---it selects the empirically best of many hypotheses, which is biased, and
bounding the bias needs uniform convergence (VC dimension, or the statistical-query
dimension $\mathrm{SQdim}$). The consequence is a graceful-degradation statement: if
search is infeasible, the verifier survives and can still certify any externally
proposed candidate.

\section{The measurable-physics bound, and the total deficit}
\label{sec:measure}

Make $D$ \textbf{finite}: $X$ is a finite list of $N$ log-space observables. Then the
candidate term is \emph{exact}---no sampling:
\[
p_g(\eps - \beta) \;=\;
\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \, \mathbf{1}\!\bigl[\, |\ln(h(x_i)/\oracle(x_i))| > \eps-\beta \,\bigr],
\]
and the certificate collapses to the oracle term alone, bounded by $m$, the number of
\emph{validated oracle outputs}---observables that are simultaneously \emph{measured}
($\cstar$ known), \emph{computable} ($\oracle$ evaluates them), and \emph{validated}
($\oracle$ calibrated against $\cstar$).

\textbf{The honest finding.} For our universe, $\cstar$ is known but $\oracle$ is not
(the compactification is unknown); for exact/tractable backgrounds, $\oracle$ is
computable but they are not our universe. The intersection---the true $m$---is
\textbf{zero} today. With $m=0$ the radius $\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta_e)/(2m)}$ is
\textbf{infinite}: the certificate is vacuous, and the verification deficit is
\emph{total}, not ``$\sim 31\%$'' as an earlier conflation of ``measured'' with
``validated'' suggested. The binding constraint is validated oracle outputs, not
compute; each observable that gains a calibrated error model moves $m$ from $0$ toward
the $\sim 10$--$19$ targets and shrinks the radius from $\infty$.

\section{The negative result: $W_0$ is log-uniform, hence not evolvable}
\label{sec:negative}

Denef--Douglas~\cite{denef} shows the flux superpotential $W_0$ is statistically
uniform in the complex plane across the flux ensemble, so the map
$\text{flux} \to W_0$ carries $\sim$zero mutual information. We measured the
distribution directly.

\textbf{Measurement.} $380$ SUSY flux vacua, sampled by JAXVacua's natural (ISD)
measure~\cite{jaxvacua}. The empirical CDF of $\log|W_0|$ is linear to $R^2 = 0.92$
(the $|W_0|^2$ CDF is not, $R^2 = 0.45$):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
variable & CDF linearity $R^2$ \\
\midrule
$\log|W_0|$ (log-uniform) & \textbf{0.923} \\
$|W_0|^2$ (area-uniform) & 0.450 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

So $|W_0|$ is \textbf{log-uniform}---the standard KKLT prior, now measured rather than
assumed. (Uniform-in-a-box sampling does \emph{not} reproduce this: $R^2 \approx
0.2$--$0.3$ regardless of box width; the natural measure is required.)

Valiant's evolvability theorem~\cite{valiant2009}: \emph{evolvable $\Rightarrow$
learnable.} A log-uniform (hence zero-information) map is not learnable, hence---by the
contrapositive---\textbf{not evolvable}: no certificate-only evolutionary search over
$W_0$ can converge. The graceful-degradation statement of Section~\ref{sec:verify}
applies: the \emph{search dies, the verifier survives.}

A direct consequence, measured: $\Pr(|W_0| < 1) = 42.6\%$ (so ``small $W_0$'' eliminates
$\sim 57\%$, not the $\sim 95\%$ that biased sampling suggested), while the KKLT target
$|W_0| \sim 10^{-4}$ lies $7.6$ nats below the observed range---its tuning probability
is an extrapolation the data cannot confirm.

\section{Empirical characterization: structure, constraints, uniformity}
\label{sec:empirical}

\textbf{Structure mining.} A non-linear miner (mutual information with permutation test,
plus a ridge-on-quadratic-features learnability test, self-validated on the
deterministic tadpole control at $r=0.99$) finds no strong geometric fingerprint in the
flux-sector observables: $W_0$ random (ridge $r=0.10$), $g_s$ weakly structured
($r=0.21$), the gauge couplings at noise ($r \le 0.17$). The geometry-pinning structure,
if it exists, lives in the observables we have not yet computed (the full spectrum with
instanton corrections).

\textbf{Constraint verification.} Treating constraints as certifiable statements---``this
constraint eliminates fraction $p$ of the sampled vacua, with confidence $1-\delta$''---we
measure, on the natural-measure sample:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
constraint & eliminates & 95\% CI \\
\midrule
weak coupling ($g_s < 1$) & 68.7\% & [59\%, 78\%] \\
small $W_0$ ($|W_0| < 1$) & 57.4\% & (measured) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A single constraint eliminates a large, certified fraction; a chain of them narrows by
elimination, which is the search-free route the framework is designed for.

\section{The boundary of the computable}
\label{sec:boundary}

The object we actually want---pin down the compactification---is uncomputable in several
distinct senses. The contribution of this paper is to \emph{name} them and certify each
as data, so that ``uncomputable'' is a map, not a wall:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
sense & meaning & the move \\
\midrule
\textbf{intractable} & answer exists, no feasible algorithm & prove intractability \\
\textbf{unmeasurable} & answer exists, data/ground-truth absent & compute the deficit \\
\textbf{underdetermined} & no unique answer exists & compute the set's invariants \\
\textbf{undecidable} & provably no algorithm & prove undecidability \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Seven questions are certified to date (search over $W_0$ = intractable; verify-today and
KKLT-tail and full-EFT and small-$\Lambda$ = unmeasurable; the measure and the inverse
problem = underdetermined). \textbf{None is undecidable}---the landscape problem is an
\emph{epistemic} and \emph{underdetermination} limit, not a Turing limit. That is itself
a finding: the path forward is not ``invent a faster computer'' but ``build the missing
oracles'' and ``compute the invariants of the underdetermined set.''

\section{Discussion and limits}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{The framework does not unify.} It does not select a vacuum; the measure problem
remains open physics. It does not eliminate oracle error; it bounds it. Every
certificate is conditional on the stipulated observable measure $D$.

\textbf{What we do not claim.} No new prediction, no measure, no unique
compactification. The negative result is scoped to the IIB flux corner and to $W_0$
($g_s$ is weakly structured; the full-spectrum observables are unmeasured). The
log-uniform prior is measured over $3.7$ nats; its extension to the KKLT tail is
explicitly an extrapolation.

\textbf{What we do claim.} A verification discipline for a theory that currently has
none: a certified deficit, a mutation-tested computation vault, a measured prior, a
negative result, and a boundary map---built in the exact gap the field's leaders
(Douglas~\cite{douglas_lean}, PhysProver~\cite{physprover}) are independently
formalizing. The two approaches---our guard/certificate discipline and their Lean
formalization---are complementary, not competing.

\section{Conclusion}

The obstacle to unified string theory is a shortage of \emph{checkable claims}, and the
deepest checkable claim in reach is not ``is this vacuum ours?'' but ``is the
compactification$\to$observables map learnable?''---answered negatively for $W_0$, and
mapped for the rest. Verification, done with Valiant's guarantees, is the honest first
move; the boundary map makes the \emph{second} move (where does computation end?) equally
honest. We have not solved the theory. We have built, tested, and measured the missing
verification layer---and the field's convergence on the same gap suggests that is the
right thing to have built.
