\title{Probably Approximately Correct String Theory:\\ A PAC Verification Framework for the String Landscape}
\author{Rob Mitchell}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
String theory's landscape contains on the order of $10^{500}$ vacua and no
experiment distinguishes most of them, so ``unified string theory'' conflates two
problems---\emph{verification} (does some vacuum match our universe?) and
\emph{selection} (why that one?). We separate them. Importing Leslie Valiant's
computational learning framework, we make verification precise as a
\emph{probably-approximately-correct certificate}: with probability $\ge 1-\delta$
a candidate vacuum's physics agrees with observation within tolerance $\eps$,
\emph{even when the verifier is itself approximate}---a two-level bound we derive.
We show single-candidate verification needs \emph{no learnability assumption},
while \emph{search} over the landscape is governed by Valiant's evolvability
theorem (\emph{evolvable $\Rightarrow$ learnable}), yielding one
necessary-condition conjecture: the compactification$\to$observables map is
SQ-learnable. Finally, making the observable measure finite collapses the sampling
term and exposes the binding constraint---verification is bounded by the number of
\emph{measurable} points of the true theory, $\sim 19$ for the Standard Model,
which today gives a certificate radius of $\sim 43\%$, not $\sim 1\%$. The
framework's product is not a solution to unification but a \emph{quantitative
statement of the verification deficit}---and a precise sense in which string theory
is, today, barely verifiable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

String theory is not, in its present form, a single theory. It is a family of
$\sim 10^{500}$ metastable vacua---the \emph{landscape}---each giving different
low-energy physics~\cite{bousso2000,douglas2003,susskind2003}. The statement
``string theory is unified'' decomposes into two claims that the literature
habitually fuses:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Verification}---there exists a vacuum whose low-energy physics
matches our universe (gauge group, spectrum, cosmological constant).
\item \textbf{Selection}---a \emph{measure} singles out that vacuum, with weight
$\sim 1$.
\end{enumerate}
The first is checkable in principle; the second is not, and it is the part that has
remained open for roughly four decades. This paper concerns only the first, and does
so rigorously.

Two facts make verification hard even as a well-posed problem. First, the search
space is astronomically large. Second, and more fundamentally, the \emph{verifier}
is not ground truth: the map from a compactification to its low-energy observables
is computed by an approximate solver---with systematic error from $\alpha'$
corrections, non-perturbative truncations, and the Kaluza--Klein tower. Any rigorous
statement about verification must therefore contend with an \emph{imperfect
labeler}.

We import the one framework built for exactly this regime. Valiant's
probably-approximately-correct (PAC) learning replaces ``exact'' with ``with
probability $\ge 1-\delta$, error $\le \eps$''~\cite{valiant1984}. We apply that
replacement to verification itself, and derive a certificate whose two error terms
are separately estimable. Three consequences follow: a clean separation of
\emph{verification} (no learnability needed) from \emph{search} (governed by
Valiant's evolvability theorem~\cite{valiant2009}); a necessary-condition conjecture
for the whole landscape program; and a bound that shows verification today is limited
not by compute but by \emph{measurable physics}.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A two-level certificate} (Theorem~\ref{thm:twolevel}): an upper bound
on the true error of a candidate vacuum that composes a candidate-vs-oracle term
with an oracle-vs-truth term, valid when the verifier is approximate.
\item \textbf{The verify/search separation} (Section~\ref{sec:verify}):
single-candidate verification needs no VC dimension; search does, and the framework
degrades gracefully when learnability fails.
\item \textbf{The measurable-physics bound} (Section~\ref{sec:measure}): a finite
observable measure collapses the sampling term, leaving a certificate radius set by
the number of \emph{measured} points---$\sim 43\%$ today, $\sim 1\%$ only with
$\sim 18{,}000$.
\item \textbf{The learnability conjecture} (Section~\ref{sec:conjecture}): the
compactification$\to$observables map is SQ-learnable; its false branch is a negative
result about the entire landscape program.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Setup and notation}

Let $X$ be the \emph{instance space}---the set of low-energy observables (masses,
couplings, the cosmological constant). Let $D$ be a probability measure over $X$:
\emph{which} observables are sampled, and with what weight. $D$ is a stipulated
scientific commitment, not given by the physics (Sections~\ref{sec:measure}
and~\ref{sec:discussion}).

Let $\cstar: X \to Y$ be the \emph{target}---the true low-energy physics of our
universe---and let $h: X \to Y$ be a candidate vacuum's predicted physics. We never
observe $\cstar$ directly; we observe it through an \emph{oracle} $\oracle: X \to Y$,
an approximate solver. Define the two disagreement functions
\[
g(x) \;=\; |h(x) - \oracle(x)|, \qquad e(x) \;=\; |\oracle(x) - \cstar(x)|,
\]
and the \emph{true error}
\[
\operatorname{err}_D(h) \;=\; \Pr_{x\sim D}\bigl[\, |h(x) - \cstar(x)| > \eps \,\bigr]
\]
for a chosen tolerance $\eps$. The goal is to certify $\operatorname{err}_D(h) \le
\delta$ for small $\delta$. We cannot observe $\operatorname{err}_D(h)$, because
$\cstar$ is reachable only through $\oracle$.

\section{The two-level certificate}
\label{sec:cert}

The triangle inequality decomposes the unobservable error into two estimable pieces:
\[
|h(x) - \cstar(x)| \;\le\; g(x) + e(x),
\]
hence, for any split point $\beta \in (0, \eps)$,
\[
\{ \, |h - \cstar| > \eps \} \;\subseteq\; \{ g > \eps - \beta \} \;\cup\; \{ e > \beta \},
\]
and, taking probabilities (a union bound),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:split}
\operatorname{err}_D(h) \;\le\; p_g(\eps - \beta) + p_e(\beta),
\end{equation}
where $p_g(\tau) = \Pr[g > \tau]$ and $p_e(\beta) = \Pr[e > \beta]$. The two terms on
the right are \emph{individually estimable}, for different reasons: $p_g$ compares two
things we can both \emph{compute} ($h$ and $\oracle$), while $p_e$ is the oracle's own
failure rate, estimable by \emph{validating} $\oracle$ on points where $\cstar$ is
known (measurement, or exact computation on tractable cases).

\begin{theorem}[Candidate bound]
\label{thm:candidate}
For a fixed $h$, sample $n$ observables $x_1,\dots,x_n \sim D$ i.i.d.\ and let
$\hat p_g$ be the empirical disagreement rate above $\eps - \beta$. Then with
probability $\ge 1 - \delta_s$,
\[
p_g(\eps - \beta) \;\le\; \hat p_g + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta_s)}{2n}} .
\]
\end{theorem}

This is a one-sided Hoeffding bound on a single proportion; no uniform-convergence
assumption, no VC dimension.

\begin{theorem}[Oracle validation bound]
\label{thm:oracle}
On $m$ independent points $z_1,\dots,z_m$ where $\cstar(z)$ is known, let $\hat p_e$
be the empirical exceedance rate of $|\oracle - \cstar|$ above $\beta$. Then with
probability $\ge 1 - \delta_e$,
\[
p_e(\beta) \;\le\; \hat p_e + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta_e)}{2m}} .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Two-level Valiant certificate]
\label{thm:twolevel}
Fix $\eps > 0$, $\beta \in (0, \eps)$, and confidence budgets
$\delta_s, \delta_e \in (0,1)$. With probability at least $1 - \delta_s - \delta_e$,
\[
\operatorname{err}_D(h) \;\le\;
\hat p_g + \hat p_e
+ \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta_s)}{2n}}
+ \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta_e)}{2m}} .
\]
\end{theorem}

The certificate is the tuple $(\eps, \beta, n, m, \hat p_g, \hat p_e, \delta_s,
\delta_e)$, from which the bound is computed. This is verification in the presence of
an imperfect labeler---the generalization of PAC's clean oracle that string theory
actually requires.

\begin{remark}[The binding constraint]
The oracle-validation radius is governed by $m$, the number of points where the true
theory is known. For the Standard Model, $m$ is bounded by the number of measured free
parameters---not by compute. This is the crux of Section~\ref{sec:measure}.
\end{remark}

\section{Verification without learnability; search with it}
\label{sec:verify}

Theorem~\ref{thm:twolevel} holds for a \emph{single, handed-to-you} hypothesis $h$. It
never invokes the complexity of the hypothesis class. That is the point:
\emph{verifying what you are given is cheap}, a Bernoulli-rate estimate.

Search is different. Search \emph{selects} the empirically best of many hypotheses,
and that selection is biased---the empirical minimum overestimates the winner.
Bounding selection bias requires uniform convergence: for a finite class, a union
bound gives radius $\sqrt{\ln(|C|/\delta)/(2n)}$; for an infinite class,
$\operatorname{VCdim}(C)$ (or the statistical-query dimension
$\operatorname{SQdim}(C)$) replaces $\ln|C|$.

The consequence is a graceful-degradation statement: if the learnability conjecture
(Section~\ref{sec:conjecture}) is false, the \emph{search} is infeasible, but the
\emph{verifier survives}---it can still certify any candidate proposed externally,
because the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:twolevel} never uses VC dimension.

\section{The observable measure and the measurable-physics bound}
\label{sec:measure}

Make $D$ \emph{finite}: $X$ is a finite, named list of $N$ observables, each
represented in log-space so that ``tolerance $\eps$'' means a uniform
\emph{multiplicative} tolerance from a gauge coupling ($O(1)$) down to the
cosmological constant ($10^{-122}$). $D$ is then a weight vector $(w_1,\dots,w_N)$.

For a finite $X$ that the oracle can fully evaluate, the candidate term is
\emph{exact}:
\[
p_g(\eps - \beta) \;=\;
\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \, \mathbf{1}\!\bigl[\, |\ln(h(x_i)/\oracle(x_i))| > \eps-\beta \,\bigr],
\]
computed directly---no sampling, no $n$. The certificate collapses to the oracle term:
\[
\operatorname{err}_D(h) \;\le\; p_g(\text{exact}) + \hat p_e
+ \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta_e)}{2m}} .
\]
The only remaining uncertainty is the oracle-validation term, bounded by $m$, the
number of measured points. With $m \sim 19$ (the Standard Model's free parameters) and
$\delta_e = 0.025$:
\[
\sqrt{\frac{\ln(40)}{2 \cdot 19}} \;\approx\; 0.31,
\]
a $\sim 31$--$43\%$ radius (the precise number depends on $m$ and the confidence
split). To drive the radius below $1\%$ requires $m \approx 18{,}000$ measured
points---\emph{four orders of magnitude more than the theory gives us}. This is the
quantitative statement of the verification deficit: string theory is today verifiable
only to $\sim$ tens of percent tolerance in the observables that matter, and the
bottleneck is measurable physics, not compute.

A second consequence is a $2\times 2$ that decides which observables matter:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & $\cstar$ known (measured) & $\cstar$ unknown \\
\midrule
$\oracle$ can compute & verification zone & prediction zone \\
$\oracle$ cannot compute & unverifiable & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Only the top-left cell tightens the certificate; the top-right cell is falsifiable
\emph{later} and contributes nothing \emph{now}. A vacuum whose only interesting
predictions lie in the top-right cell is scientifically sterile \emph{today},
regardless of elegance.

\section{Search and the learnability conjecture}
\label{sec:conjecture}

Valiant's evolvability model~\cite{valiant2009} restricts selection to
\emph{statistical-query} access to the target: fitness is read only through aggregate
statistics, never per-instance ground truth. This restriction is not imposed here for
tidiness---it is \emph{already how the search works}, because the only access a search
has to a vacuum's fitness is the certificate of Theorem~\ref{thm:twolevel} (sampled
rates with confidence radii). Valiant's theorem is:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Evolvable $\Rightarrow$ learnable.} Any concept class evolvable in the
restricted model is learnable in the corresponding statistical-query model.
\end{quote}

Its contrapositive is the gate on search:

\begin{conjecture}[Bounded SQ dimension]
\label{conj:sqdim}
The class $C = \{ h_v : X \to Y : v \in V \}$ of observable-prediction functions
induced by vacua has \emph{polynomial SQ dimension}---i.e., the
compactification$\to$observables map is SQ-learnable.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{True} $\Rightarrow$ the landscape is \emph{evolvable}: an evolutionary
search with certificate-only access and provable guarantees can find a near-optimal
vacuum.
\item \textbf{False} $\Rightarrow$ no search can find our vacuum---a negative result
about the entire landscape program.
\end{itemize}

Either branch is a finding, and the conjecture is stated so it is attackable on small
vacuum classes before anyone touches $10^{500}$.

\section{Discussion and limits}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{The framework does not unify.} It does not select a vacuum---the measure
(Section~\ref{sec:intro}, claim~2) is untouched and remains the 40-year-open physics
problem. It does not eliminate oracle error---it bounds it. It does not derive
$D$---every certificate is conditional on the stipulated observable measure. And it
does not turn a surrogate into truth: no error model, no certificate.

\textbf{The imperfect oracle is the through-line.} It appears in
Theorem~\ref{thm:twolevel} (verification) and again in
Section~\ref{sec:conjecture} (search, where fitness is read through certificates). A
fully general treatment---\emph{evolvability with an imperfect performance oracle}---is
the natural extension and is left open.

\textbf{What is buildable vs.\ not.} All of Sections~\ref{sec:cert}--\ref{sec:conjecture}
is software (a reference implementation accompanies this paper). The measure problem,
and the full EFT extraction that a non-trivial learnability measurement requires, are
physics research, not tooling.

\section{Conclusion}

The obstacle to unified string theory is not a shortage of cleverness but a shortage
of \emph{checkable claims}---and the deepest checkable claim in reach is not ``is this
vacuum ours?'' but ``is the compactification$\to$observables map learnable?'' Answer
that, and one knows whether the landscape is searchable at all. Verification, done
with Valiant's guarantees, is the honest first move; selection is the hard second, and
no amount of the first buys the second. The framework's contribution is to make the
first move \emph{rigorous} and to state, with a number, how far we are from being able
to verify any of it.
