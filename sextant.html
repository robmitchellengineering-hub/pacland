How it works

Five steps, and not every claim clears them

  1. Frame — state a claim, its assumptions and its domain.
  2. Generate — propose candidate consequences or connections.
  3. Falsify — attempt to break the claim with a concrete test.
  4. Verify — use formal proof or reproducible evidence appropriate to the claim.
  5. Record — preserve provenance, limitations and current epistemic status.

A simulation is not a formal proof, and a claim that survives one stage may still fail the next. Each claim below says which stage it has reached and which it has not.

Verdict vocabulary

Three verdicts, scoped to their assumptions

FORCED — follows from the stated assumptions/structure. The formal proof and its exact scope are shown.

FREE — not determined by the stated constraints. This does not mean no deeper theory could ever explain it.

IMPOSSIBLE — ruled out under the stated assumptions. The no-go argument and its domain are shown.

These labels describe the relationship between a claim and its stated assumptions — not unrestricted statements about the whole universe.

    Case study

    The Yang–Mills mass gap — a research programme

    The mass-gap work is presented here as a research programme, not a finished result. The question under investigation is whether a non-abelian gauge theory develops a positive spectral gap above its vacuum. The programme proposes a chain of lemmas and assumptions — spectral-gap, Lyapunov-drift and mode-sum arguments — and separates them honestly:

    Formally checked: the individual lemmas that carry a Lean proof, and only those.

    Computationally tested: the numerical and empirical evidence that supports (or stresses) the conjectured chain.

    Conjectural / open: the full existence-and-mass-gap theorem, and any step that has not yet been reduced to a checked lemma.

    Status, said plainly: the Yang–Mills mass gap has not been solved. Internal proof-checking is not proof of the full target theorem, the complete proof has not been independently validated, and the mathematical community has not accepted it. Claims here are bounded to exactly what has been checked or tested.

    Cross-field discovery

    The intended methodology, and its honest status

    • A known-unifications library that recognises established connections.
    • An open-transfer frontier for genuinely new candidate links.
    • Typed scientific structures — definitions, assumptions, domains, properties, evidence, limitations and permitted mappings.
    • An encoding-check gate, so a formally valid but scientifically empty mapping is not called a discovery.
    • A candidate must yield a new prediction, proof technique or more efficient experiment.
    • Blind proposal and external expert assessment are part of the success criterion.

    Status (per the assessment log): the core loop and the cross-field machinery are built — cross-reference, transfer verification, the known-unifications library (282+ known links), the constraint-first economics work, the novelty-search loop, and the solvers. What remains a dependency is empirical validation and partner work, and genuine novelty stays rare by design.

    Working example

    Constraint-first economics — soft fields, hard checks

    The same method that labels a physics claim FORCED / FREE / IMPOSSIBLE is applied to economics: state the structure, derive what follows from it, and record what remains free. Precise unifications let a soft field inherit the same verifiability as hard science — every claim carries its assumptions, its falsifier and its epistemic tier, and the encoding-check gate refuses a mapping that is formally valid but scientifically empty.

    This is the clearest “product in action” outside pure physics: the known-unifications library recognises established connections, and a candidate is only counted when it yields a new prediction, a proof technique, or a more efficient experiment. See the plan and the assessment log for the current state.

    Roadmap

    What works now, what is partial, what is open

    Working now: the claim registry, falsifiers, oracles, evidence and per-claim downloads on this page; the headless sextant CLI and sextant-node.

    Partial / experimental: the formal proof arm — some lemmas are kernel-checked, others are derived or conjectural; treat each claim's own column as the authority.

    Planned: empirical validation and partner work; wiring the live claim registry into this page.

    Open research questions: the Yang–Mills mass gap, and every claim whose verdict is not yet backed by a checked proof.

    Install

    Run it on your own hardware

    SEXTANT is a headless command — no cloud, no account, no trust required.

    curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/robmitchellengineering-hub/pacland/main/scripts/install-sextant.sh | bash
    sextant --verify    # every claim — every falsifier runs
sextant-node        # the headless node at http://127.0.0.1:8090

    Manual install: git clone https://github.com/robmitchellengineering-hub/pacland && cd pacland && pip install -e . stim quimb numpy.
